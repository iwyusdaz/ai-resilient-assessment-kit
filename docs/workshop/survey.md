# Workshop survey — pre and post

**Same items before and after.** Five confidence items (5-point Likert) + two open questions. Anonymous; collect the pre-survey via the calendar-invite link or in minutes 0–2, the post-survey in minutes 82–88. Run it as a form (LMS/Forms) or print both halves of this page.

## Part 1 — Confidence items (5 = strongly agree … 1 = strongly disagree)

Rate yourself right now:

1. **I can look at one of my own assessments and judge which rubric criteria generative AI satisfies easily.**
2. **I can set an explicit AI-use level for each stage of an assignment, and explain the choice to students.**
3. **I know at least three practical redesign moves for AI-era assessment, with their workload costs.**
4. **I could redesign one of my own assignments this month so the grade certifies something AI cannot produce alone.**
5. **I can explain to a student why our AI-use rules are what they are, without hiding behind a detector.**

> Post-survey wording change (only): "I can" → "After this workshop, I can". Everything else is identical — that is what makes the pre/post comparison meaningful.

## Part 2 — Open questions

6. **What is the one assignment you most want to redesign, and what is stopping you?**
   *(Pre-survey. The post-survey asks: "What will you do differently with that assignment in the next two weeks?")*
7. **What would make this redesignable for you — time, policy coverage, department support, examples from your discipline?**

## Data handling

- Anonymous by design: no names, no emails, no free-text asking for identifying details.
- Aggregate reporting only (pre/post means per item, themes from open text).
- If you collect canvases with workshop permission, store them separately from survey data.
- Share the summary back with participants — the loop closes when they see what changed.

## What this survey can and cannot show

- **Can show:** self-reported confidence change after one session; barriers named by participants.
- **Cannot show:** whether redesigns actually happen (that's the two-week commitment follow-up), or student learning effects. Say this in your summary — it models the honesty the kit teaches.
