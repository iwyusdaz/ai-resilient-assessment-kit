# Participant handout

One page, printed or on your device. Keep it beside you through the session.

## 1. The five AI-use levels (AIAS 2.1 — icon + text, never icon alone)

| Level | Name | One-line meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No AI** | Nothing generative, at any stage of this part |
| 2 | **AI Planning** | AI may help plan and brainstorm, before the real work |
| 3 | **AI Collaboration** | AI may critique and suggest during the work; the student decides, verifies, and logs |
| 4 | **Full AI** | AI may generate freely; the log and the student's evaluation of it are graded |
| 5 | **AI Exploration** | Co-creating with AI *is* the task; reflection is the evidence |

Source: Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh — AI Assessment Scale, version 2.1 (levels verified on the scale's official site, 2026-10-25; see the kit's literature notes).

## 2. The two lanes (after University of Sydney)

- **Secure lane (small, weighted):** in-class checks, defences, vivas. Certifies the individual achievement. No AI here.
- **Open lane (most of the work):** practice, drafts, notebooks, feedback. AI allowed at a named level with disclosure. This is where learning happens.

Weights that work (Case B): open practice 10% · secure checks 20%.

## 3. The criterion-risk test

For each rubric criterion, ask: *could a competent AI produce this at standard, without the learning?*

- **Pass** — AI does it easily → redesign or reweight
- **Fragile** — AI approximates it → raise the bar (verification, personal context)
- **Holds** — needs the student's process or live defence → keep, make visible
