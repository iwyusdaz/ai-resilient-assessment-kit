# Case C — the original: PSY 250 research report

**Context (fictional):** Lakeside State University — a fictional US public university preparing for ADA Title II digital accessibility compliance. PSY 250 *Research Methods in Psychology* is a second-year core course with ~120 students. The brief below is realistic but fictional; no real course or student data are used.

## The original assignment brief

> ### PSY 250 — Lab Report (30% of course grade)
>
> **Task.** Write a full research report (2,000 words) on the dataset provided: a fictional study of 150 participants testing whether a brief mindfulness exercise reduces measured anxiety before a public-speaking task. The dataset (CSV on the LMS) includes: condition (mindfulness/control), pre- and post-task anxiety scores, and demographics.
>
> Your report must follow standard structure: **Abstract, Introduction, Method, Results, Discussion, References** (APA 7).
>
> - Introduction: 2–3 paragraphs positioning the study in the literature.
> - Method: describe the design and measures as given.
> - Results: report the appropriate statistical test with APA-style statistics and a figure.
> - Discussion: interpret, note one limitation, and suggest a follow-up.
> - Use at least 6 scholarly references.
>
> **Submission.** PDF via the LMS, week 12.
>
> **Academic integrity.** The dataset is provided by the course; analyses must be your own. Quoting statistics software output without interpretation is insufficient. Generative AI tools may not be used for any part of this report.

## The original rubric (30 points)

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Introduction & literature grounding | 6 |
| Method description (design, measures, procedure) | 5 |
| Results (correct test, APA statistics, figure) | 7 |
| Discussion (interpretation, limitation, follow-up) | 7 |
| Writing & APA formatting | 5 |

## What the teaching team actually does with it

- Grading is heavy: ~45 minutes per report across TAs; calibration sessions help but Results-section marking (is the test right? is the figure right?) dominates the time.
- Every semester, a handful of Reports discuss "the mindfulness intervention" with verbatim-identical phrasing across students, and at least one discusses results that are in the file but were never computed by the student.
- The integrity line prohibits AI outright; no one believes it works.

## Why this case matters

The lab report is the archetypal "authentic task" — except when the data is provided by the instructor, the authentic part is analysing and writing about data the student has no relationship with. Generative models handle exactly that: provided-data summary in, methods-results-discussion out. The redesign makes the data genuinely the students' own and grades the decisions, not the prose.
