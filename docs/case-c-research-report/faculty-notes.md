# Case C — faculty notes

Cost, scaling and equity notes for the [redesign](redesign.html). Setting is the fictional PSY 250 at Lakeside State University — a fictional US public university preparing for ADA Title II digital accessibility compliance — ~120 students in tutorials of 20, one coordinator and three TAs.

## Grading time

| Component | Original | Redesign | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-report marking | ~45 min × 120 (≈ 90 h/term) | **~13.5–14.5 min per student total** (≈ 27–29 h) | Rows below sum to the total |
| Notebook | — | ~3 min (checklist + 1 spot-check of two entries) | The spot-check looks for dated, problem-centred entries |
| Memo (500 words) | — | ~4 min | Criterion 3; the Q&A covers it again per student |
| Report | (the 45 min above) | ~4–5 min | Marked on structure/accuracy/interpretation; analysis correctness is re-checked only where the memo and Q&A disagree with it |
| Q&A | — | ~2 min per student (10-minute team session in tutorial) | Three anchors per member: method / result-or-limitation / next step |
| Twist registration | — | ~0.5 min per student (the coordinator reviews all 24 team registrations in one ≈ 1 h batch) | Amortised: ≈ 2.5 min per team |

**Where the grade weight comes from:** nowhere new — the redesigned components' raw weights sum to 38 points, and the course handbook caps the task at 30% of the final grade, so every component scales by 30/38 (shown in the [rubric's gradebook mapping](rubric.html)). The original task also carried 30%.

**Net:** roughly 30% of the original marking load (≈ 27–29 h vs ≈ 90 h), with better assurance — because the Q&A replaces the impossible task of verifying every student's statistics by re-reading prose.

## Scaling to 200+ students

1. **Team sessions, not individual orals.** One 10-minute team Q&A inside each tutorial covers 5 students ≈ 2 min/student. Four tutorials per TA ≈ two Q&A sessions per TA per week for two weeks.
2. **Notebook spot-checks, not audits.** Check every notebook against the checklist (~3 min), deep-read two entries from every fifth team. Q&A performance drives who gets looked at more closely.
3. **One shared protocol, 24 team twists.** The coordinator reviews twist registrations in one batch against three approved-variation rules (same measures; no new populations; no sensitive topics). Anything outside → team uses the standard variant (still fully gradeable).
4. **Report marking stays conventional** — TAs calibrate on three reports per section before marking week.
5. If TAs are cut: fold the Q&A to 6 minutes per team (one question per member), and grade the memo by checklist. Never cut the notebook spot-check — it's what makes the Q&A honest.

## Accessibility and accommodations

- **The Q&A has three equal-weight modes** (live / recorded / written, ~250 words per question), chosen once at term start. Students with communication disabilities, anxiety or speaking accommodations are never required to speak — no diagnosis disclosure needed.
- **The data-collection step has a provided-dataset fallback** for anyone who cannot collect (remote students, care duties, disability that makes approaching strangers costly, visa restrictions on recruitment). The fallback dataset is written to have the same *shape* of messiness (missing responses, one skewed variable) so the notebook criterion stays meaningful.
- **Notebook format is flexible** — text, bullets, audio-transcribed, screen-reader-friendly templates provided. Grading criteria never depend on format.
- **Team roles are chosen, not assigned**, so a student's accommodation (e.g., not handling recruitment) can be a legitimate role; tagged notebook entries make each member's contribution explicit.
- All briefs, the question bank and templates ship in accessible formats (same baseline as this site: [README accessibility section](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/ai-resilient-assessment-kit#accessibility)).

## Multilingual students

- The Q&A's written mode and the notebook's format freedom remove the oral-English penalty.
- **AI language help in the report is explicitly allowed with disclosure** (AIAS level 3) — multilingual students get to write in their strongest language, get editing support openly, and are assessed on analysis and defence, not idiom.
- Memo and notebook are graded on meaning: a plain, precise entry beats fluent vagueness.
- No detectors anywhere in the marking chain (Liang et al. 2023; Weber-Wulff et al. 2023) — multilingual students are the population detectors harm most, and this redesign gives them no reason to be near one.

## Common pushback, with answers

- **"Letting undergraduates collect data is a governance nightmare."** The shared topic runs on the department's standing class-study approval; team twists are constrained to pre-approved variation (same measures, no new populations). One coordinator reviews 24 registrations in an hour.
- **"The report is now AI-tolerant — that lowers standards."** The report stops being the assurance mechanism (the memo, notebook and Q&A are). Grading the prose *while knowing* AI can write it was the false standard; this one is honest about what each part certifies.
- **"Teams mean free-riding."** Tagged notebook entries + per-member Q&A + role choice make contribution visible. Not airtight — but the original's individual report had its own unenforced fiction.

## What we would measure after the first run

- Grading minutes per component vs. the estimates above.
- Fallback-dataset uptake (if >15%, collection design needs revisiting).
- Q&A mode distribution and score parity across modes (watching for a written-mode gap — the equity canary).
