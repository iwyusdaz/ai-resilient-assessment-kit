# Case A — the original: SOC 101 argumentative essay

**Context (fictional):** Lakeside State University — a fictional US public university preparing for ADA Title II digital accessibility compliance. SOC 101 *Introduction to Sociology* is a first-year general-education course with 180 students, run by one lecturer and two TAs. This brief is realistic but fictional; no real course or student work is used.

## The original assignment brief

> ### SOC 101 — Essay 2 (25% of course grade)
>
> **Task.** Write an argumentative essay of **1,500 words** on the following question:
>
> *Does social media use harm adolescent mental health?*
>
> Take a clear position and defend it. Your essay must:
>
> - State a thesis in the introduction and sustain it throughout.
> - Use **at least five scholarly sources** (peer-reviewed journal articles or academic books), cited in APA 7 style.
> - Engage with at least one counter-argument.
> - Conclude with implications, not a summary.
>
> **Submission.** A single Word or PDF document via the LMS, 11:59 pm Friday, week 9.
>
> **Academic integrity.** Plagiarism or use of AI writing tools will be investigated under the university's academic misconduct policy.
>
> **Rubric.** See below (20 points).

## The original rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 5 (excellent) | 3–4 (good) | 1–2 (weak) | 0 (absent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis & argument** (5) | Clear, contestable thesis; sustained, logically ordered argument | Clear thesis; mostly consistent argument | Vague thesis; argument drifts | No discernible thesis |
| **Use of evidence** (5) | ≥5 relevant scholarly sources, accurately cited and integrated | 5 sources, mostly relevant; minor citation issues | Fewer than 5 or mostly non-scholarly sources | No scholarly evidence |
| **Analysis & depth** (5) | Counter-argument addressed; implications drawn; nuance shown | Some analysis beyond description; counter-argument present | Mostly summary or assertion | Descriptive only |
| **Writing & organization** (5) | Fluent, well-organized, error-free APA 7 | Clear writing; minor errors | Disorganized; frequent errors | Unintelligible |

## What the teaching team actually does with it

- The two TAs grade ~90 essays each against this rubric, in about 15–20 minutes per essay.
- Turnitin's similarity score is checked; there is no AI-detection policy beyond the sentence above.
- The same question has been used for six semesters. Instructors report increasing suspicion about "essays that don't sound like my students" but no formal review of the task since 2023.

## Why this case matters

This is the most common assignment shape in higher education: a take-home essay marked against a generic rubric. If generative AI can satisfy this rubric, the grade no longer certifies what it claims to certify. The [stress test](stress-test.html) shows which criteria are at risk; the [redesign](redesign.html) rebuilds the task around them.
