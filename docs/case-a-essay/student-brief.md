# Case A — the assignment as students see it

*This is the brief students receive. It is written in plain language. The AI-use level of every stage is shown with an icon and its text label, and the example disclosure statement at the end shows what honest AI use looks like.*

## SOC 101 Essay 2 — "Does social media harm adolescent mental health?"

You'll build this essay in five small pieces instead of one big scramble. Each piece gets a clear AI rule. The rules are about **learning, not policing**: some parts of this task build your brain, and some parts AI can genuinely help with — the level for each part tells you which is which.

**The five stages and what each one is worth**

| Stage | Due | Worth | AI level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Proposal | Week 5 | 5% | **AI Planning** — AI can help you plan |
| 2. Annotated source list | Week 7 | 10% | **No AI** — you find and read these yourself |
| 3. Draft + AI-use log | Week 8 | complete/incomplete | **AI Collaboration** — AI feedback allowed, logged |
| 4. Final essay | Week 10 | 15% | **No AI** for new writing |
| 5. Your defence (5 min) | Week 10–11 | 5% | **No AI** — you answer, live or recorded |

<span class="aias aias-2" aria-hidden="true"></span> **Stage 1 — Proposal (5%).** One page: your position, your thesis in one sentence, and two counter-arguments worth taking seriously. **AI level 2 · AI Planning:** you may use AI to brainstorm counter-arguments or outline. Write down in your own words which position *you* commit to — AI doesn't get to pick.

<span class="aias aias-1" aria-hidden="true"></span> **Stage 2 — Annotated source list (10%).** Find **five scholarly sources** (peer-reviewed articles or academic books) yourself. For each one: the full APA 7 reference, a ~120-word annotation in your own words, and a **verification note** — how you got the full text (which database or the library), plus one direct quotation with its page number. **AI level 1 · No AI:** no AI-generated references, summaries or annotations. We check sources — and AI still invents references that don't exist, so this protects your grade.

<span class="aias aias-3" aria-hidden="true"></span> **Stage 3 — Draft + AI-use log (complete/incomplete).** Write a full draft and hand it in with a one-page log: every time you used AI, what you asked, what you kept, what you threw out and why. **AI level 3 · AI Collaboration:** AI may give feedback on your draft — clarity, flow, counter-arguments you've missed. It may not write passages for you. Graded for completion only: an honest log full of "AI suggested X, I rejected it because Y" scores the same as a short one.

<span class="aias aias-1" aria-hidden="true"></span> **Stage 4 — Final essay (15%).** 1,500 words, revised from your draft. **AI level 1 · No AI** for new writing: the essay must be your words. (Your own draft carries forward — that's the point of stage 3.) Same rubric dimensions as the old essay: argument, evidence, analysis, writing.

<span class="aias aias-1" aria-hidden="true"></span> **Stage 5 — Your defence (5%).** Five minutes, in your tutorial **or recorded on your phone/LMS, or written** (three questions, ~250 words each — your choice, all count the same). We'll ask from a published question bank: how you found one source, why you chose your framing over the counter-argument, and one thing you changed after feedback or AI critique. **AI level 1 · No AI** — this is the part that certifies the thinking is yours.

## Why these rules (the honest version)

- AI can already produce a fluent essay on this topic. That's fine — a fluent essay was never the actual goal. The goal is that **you** can find real evidence, reason from it, and defend it. The stages make that visible.
- **We don't use AI detectors.** They misfire — including against writers whose first language isn't English — and OpenAI retired its own detector for inaccuracy. Your honest disclosure log is worth more than any detector, and nobody is penalised for honest AI use at the stated levels.
- If life gets in the way, talk to us **before** the deadline. An honest late proposal beats an AI-polished silent scramble.

## Your AI disclosure statement

Stages 2, 4 and 5 have no AI use to declare (that's the rule). Stages 1 and 3 (and anything else you used AI for) get one short paragraph at the end of the submission, like this example:

> **AI use statement.** For this essay I used ChatGPT (free tier) at AI Planning level: I asked it for possible counter-arguments to my thesis and listed two in my plan. For the draft I worked without AI (No AI). After the draft I used the university's AI feedback tool at AI Collaboration level to check paragraph clarity; I rewrote two paragraphs in my own words where it suggested changes I disagreed with. I verified every source by opening and reading it — AI suggested none of them.

You can be honest about *more* AI use, not less: an overstated disclosure costs you nothing but a conversation.

## Quick checklist before you submit each stage

- Did I check the AI level for this stage?
- If AI level 2 or 3: is my disclosure paragraph written?
- Stage 2 only: did I open and read every source myself?
- Does every file have my name, my tutorial group and the stage number?

Questions? Ask early. The whole point of the levels is that you never have to guess.
