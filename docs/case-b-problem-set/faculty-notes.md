# Case B — faculty notes

Cost, scaling and equity notes for the [redesign](redesign.html). Setting is the fictional STAT 201 at Lakeside State University — a fictional US public university preparing for ADA Title II digital accessibility compliance — 250 students, one coordinator, four TAs.

## Grading time

| Component | Original | Redesign | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly sets ×5 | 10–12 min × 250 × 5 weeks (≈ 90–100 h per term across TAs) | **~1.5 min × 250 × 5 (≈ 19 h)** — completion + log check | The answer key stops mattering; the log is 5 lines |
| Secure checks ×2 | (nothing equivalent — midterm only) | ~4 min × 250 × 2 (≈ 33 h) | Four questions each; Q4 is 1 mark; marks fast |
| Net | ≈ 90–100 h | ≈ 52 h + calibration (≈ 4 h) | **About half the original marking load**, with far more assurance |

The design trades marking answers for marking logs and short checks. If logs feel too light, the alternative is sampling deep-read 10% of sets per week (adds ≈ 8 h).

## Scaling to 200+ students

1. The secure checks run **inside existing tutorial slots** — no invigilation hiring, no room booking. Sections of 30 take 20 minutes with the TA present; nothing else fits their slot that week.
2. Log grading is binary (complete/partial) — a checklist, not an essay mark. New TAs calibrate on 15 sample logs in one 45-minute session.
3. The class dataset is collected **once** (week 2, LMS form) and cleaned by the coordinator; every section uses the same file. No per-section variation to manage.
4. "Find and fix" items are pre-written (from last year's anonymised errors); adding one to a set costs nothing.
5. If TAs are scarce, the checks can be marked on a 20-point scheme by Q4 auto-marking (LMS quiz format for Q1 and Q4, hand-marked for Q2–Q3) — about 2 min/script.

## Accessibility and accommodations

- **Secure checks:** any student with extended-time or rest-break accommodations takes the check with the TA's standard arrangement; because the check is only 20 minutes, doubling time still fits a tutorial slot.
- **Missed check:** a make-up slot in the same week; a second miss converts to a 15-minute oral equivalent (or written Q&A) with the coordinator — accommodations don't require disclosure of reasons.
- **Open lane:** logs can be voice notes, bullet points, or table rows — format doesn't matter, honesty does. Screen-reader users and students with RSI can use AI more, not less: the levels never *require* handwriting, and the tutorial checks have a typed equal-weight alternative per the university's accommodation process.
- **The class survey** is optional to answer (it informs the dataset, not your grade); students who opt out use the anonymised dataset like everyone else. No sensitive questions; the collection form is reviewed by the teaching team against the university's minimal-data guidance.

## Multilingual students

- Interpretation-in-context sentences are graded on **meaning, not idiom** — a plain, slightly awkward sentence that gets the numbers and limits right scores full marks.
- The open lane explicitly legitimises AI language help ("ask AI to explain the question in simpler words" is a logged, allowed use) — which is exactly the support multilingual students have been using silently under the old ban.
- Detector use stays off the table: the evidence on false positives for non-native writers (Liang et al. 2023; Weber-Wulff et al. 2023) is the reason the redesign needs no detector.
- The formula sheet may include the student's own brief wording of each formula in any language — transcription into English is not being tested.

## Common pushback, with answers

- **"Full AI on problem sets is surrender."** The sets stop certifying anything; the checks certify. The alternative — banning AI from unenforceable take-home sets — certifies nothing and manufactures suspicion.
- **"Two 20-minute checks can't measure everything."** Correct. They measure the four highest-priority skills, weighted honestly (20% > the practice lane's 10%), and the midterm remains.
- **"Students will split logs — write the log after copying answers."** Some will; that's why the checks exist and why the log grades judgment (catching an AI error) rather than compliance. The redesign doesn't claim to make everyone honest; it makes honesty the cheap, safe strategy and makes certainty possible on the secure lane.

## What we would measure after the first term

- Check-score correlation with midterm score (validity signal for the checks).
- Log-quality distribution and its relationship to check performance (does engaged AI use predict secure-lane scores?).
- TA hours actually spent vs. the estimates above.
