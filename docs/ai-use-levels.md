# AI-use levels, explained

This kit uses the **AI Assessment Scale (AIAS) 2.1** (Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh) — a five-level scale that replaces vague "may I use AI?" rules with an explicit level for each part of a task. The level names below are the current AIAS 2.1 names, verified against the scale's official site on 2026-10-25.

## The five levels

Each level appears everywhere in this kit as a **badge: icon + text** (never an icon alone, so it works for screen readers and in plain text).

Badge styling is provided by the kit's stylesheet; in the raw markdown you will see the levels as plain text ("**AI level 3 · AI Collaboration**") so nothing depends on colour or icons alone.

## Setting a level per stage, not per course

The level belongs to the *part* of the task, because different stages serve different learning goals. A typical pattern in this kit:

| Stage | Typical level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a topic, brainstorming | AI Planning (2) | Ideas are cheap; commitment to one is the student's decision |
| Finding and verifying sources | No AI (1) | Verifying that sources exist is a core academic capability (and AI fabricates citations) |
| Producing the artefact | No AI (1) or AI Collaboration (3) | Depends on whether the course outcome includes AI-assisted production |
| Getting feedback on a draft | AI Collaboration (3) | Feedback-from-AI mirrors real professional practice, with critique required |
| Defending choices (oral/written check) | No AI (1) | This is the secure lane — the point is the student's own reasoning |
| Reflecting on AI use | AI Planning (2)–AI Exploration (5) | Honest disclosure is assessed, not penalised |

## What students must disclose, and how

Disclosure is required at levels 2–5 and takes one short paragraph at the end of the submission (template: [assignment-ai-statement](templates/assignment-ai-statement.html)):

1. **Which level** each part of the task used.
2. **Which tool(s)**, by name, for what.
3. **What you changed** — where you rejected, rewrote or verified AI output.
4. **One thing you learned or would do differently.**

Example disclosure statement (also shown in every [student brief](case-a-essay/student-brief.html)):

> **AI use statement.** For this essay I used ChatGPT (free tier) at AI Planning level: I asked it for possible counter-arguments to my thesis and listed two in my plan. For the draft I worked without AI (No AI). After the draft I used the university's AI feedback tool at AI Collaboration level to check paragraph clarity; I rewrote two paragraphs in my own words where it suggested changes I disagreed with. I verified every source by opening and reading it — AI suggested none of them.

## Why the rules exist (the version we tell students)

The rules are about **learning, not policing**: the level for each stage is set so that *you* do the parts of the work that build your capability, and AI does the parts that don't. AI detectors are not used in this course — they misfire, including against multilingual writers — so the honesty of the disclosure log is what matters. This mirrors the position in the [kit principles](principles.html) (principles 2 and 6).
